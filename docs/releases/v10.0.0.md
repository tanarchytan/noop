A major version for the size of the change underneath, not for anything you have to do. The app speaks
Spanish, Rhythm Age finally has somewhere to appear, and four numbers that were quietly answering the
wrong question now answer the right one.

## Fixed

- **HRV was under-reported.** R-R beats sharing a second were read in magnitude order rather than the
  order the strap emitted them. RMSSD is built from the difference between consecutive beats and is
  computed within a second, so that ordering is its entire input — and sorting shrinks adjacent
  differences by construction. Measured across five straps: 25% low on this fork's own 5.0 (23.5 ms
  against 29.3), 17% on another user's. Beats are now stamped with their emission order at decode.
  Nights recorded before this keep their stored values — the order was never recorded and cannot be
  recovered, so nothing is silently rewritten.
- **Resting heart rate was a floor, not a resting level.** It had been the lowest sustained stretch the
  night could hold, which is the bottom of a distribution rather than the middle of one. It is now the
  median in-bed level, which is what the published figure it gets compared against measures. On the
  nights where both exist the floor read 10.84 bpm low and the median lands at +0.94, mean absolute
  error 1.06. HRV over the same nights was +1.33 ms, so the reported gap between NOOP and a reference
  band was never HRV — it was this. One wearer, so the floor stays available as the instrument that
  measured it.
- **Restorative Sleep could be earned on a short night.** Rest was scored as a share of whatever was
  slept, so a night with good architecture and not enough of it scored like a full one. It is scored in
  minutes against your need now, and the diagnostic under it mirrors the scorer rather than restating
  the old share. On a five-hour-ten night that moves Rest from about 81 to about 74. Stored history and
  the baselines built over it recompute on one generation rather than leaving old and new side by side.
- **Restoring onto an empty install had no way to recover.** Both of the importer's fallbacks needed a
  live store to copy into, so a migration failure was quietly rescued on a phone that had been used and
  fatal on a fresh one — and a fresh phone is when people restore. It now creates the empty store this
  version expects and copies rows into that. Two schema probes also opened the payload read-only with
  no fallback, which is the one thing a write-ahead-log file with no companion index cannot do. Failures
  now carry a reason code, and the log carries the incoming store's version, table count and size, so a
  report from a phone we do not hold can be diagnosed.
- **Effort was blank on a day Charge was not.** Charge carried a previously scored day forward and
  Effort did not, so an import whose newest day is older than today filled one ring and left the other
  empty.
- **The unit switches only took effect on the next launch.** Metric/Imperial, °C/°F and the Effort scale
  were read once at startup rather than held as state, so flipping one reached the screen you were on
  and nothing else. Two sites also formatted a temperature by hand and bypassed the switch entirely, and
  the deviation formatter dropped the sign, so a rise above your norm read as an absolute reading.
- **A sleep edit left the night disagreeing with itself.** Correcting a bed or wake time reclipped the
  stages but kept the efficiency computed from the old window, so the night's own figure and the day's
  no longer described the same sleep. Measured on a real store: every one of eleven hand-edited nights
  disagreed, and none of nineteen untouched ones did. Both now come from one formula.
- **A day recorded by two straps lost one of them.** Where two straps both covered a day, the merge
  kept whichever sorted first and discarded the other row entire — so if the winner happened to be the
  emptier of the two, the day rendered with no sleep, no recovery, no resting heart rate and no HRV
  while a complete record sat beside it. The day is now assembled column by column from both.
- **Daily calories were the whole day, in a field that means active.** The figure included the energy
  you burn lying still, while the phone series it fills in, the coach line and the ring all read it as
  active energy. It now stores the active half, so the number drops.
- **WHOOP 4.0 blood oxygen is withheld.** The red/IR pair arrives once a second, so the fastest signal
  it can represent is 0.5 Hz while a pulse runs 0.83 to 3 Hz: the pulsatile component the calculation
  reads is gone before decode. Confirmed on two straps and 2.1 million samples — 89% and 98% of
  windows carried no pulsation, and the survivors produced ~80%, which would be severe hypoxaemia in
  two healthy people. It also no longer leaves the device through Health Connect.
- **The raw sensor export produced an empty file** for any strap paired through the wizard: it asked
  for the legacy device id instead of the active one. Its window also covered 24 hours against an
  offload carrying about a fortnight.
- **Backups from different builds could delete each other.** Snapshots from the debug and mock builds
  were named identically to the release app's, and pruning counted them as one pool.

## Added

- **A Spanish interface, and a language setting to choose it.** Every user-visible string in the app is
  a resource now — 2,335 of them across fourteen files — and 2,295 have a Spanish translation. The forty
  that stay English are NOOP's own metric names (Charge, Effort, Rest, Recovery, Strain), the clinical
  abbreviations (HRV, REM) and the short source tags on the workouts filter. Profile → Language holds
  System default, English and Español, and applies without going through the phone's own language list.
  A partial German layer inherited from upstream is still on disk and still applies to a phone set to
  German, but it covers 120 strings, so it is not offered as a choice — choosing a language should mean
  the app is in it.
- **Wheel pickers everywhere a number is set.** Waist, max heart rate and both step-calibration values in
  Profile, the sitting-for, re-nudge and buzz-strength settings in Automations, the alarm wake window and
  the interval timer's work, rest and round counts. Spinning to a value replaces tapping a plus sign
  eighty times, and max heart rate gained the one thing its stepper could not express: handing it back to
  Auto once you have set it by hand. The interval wheels are inert while a session runs.
- **Movement on the night's chart, beside heart rate.** The sleep chart already drew heart rate across
  the stages; it now draws per-epoch movement on the same axis in a second colour — heart rate rose,
  movement cyan, with a key underneath. The two together are what the stager reads, so a boundary you
  disagree with is visible rather than asserted.
- **Restore is reachable during setup.** Loading a `.noopbak` was only in Settings, which comes after the
  walkthrough, so the one moment a fresh install most needs its history had no route to it. The
  walkthrough now offers it directly.
- **Rhythm Age has a card**, in Health under Body signals. It reads the shape of your daily rest and
  activity from on-wrist motion and expresses it as an age, charted against your real age and your
  fitness age. It needs seven worn days before it first appears and counts up until then, so the wait
  is visible rather than silent. A wellness estimate, best read as a trend — not a medical result, and
  not a life expectancy.
- **The battery pack on the Devices screen.** Its charge sits on its own gauge under the strap's, its
  serial and firmware under the strap's firmware. There is nothing to pair: the pack reaches the strap
  over NFC and the strap serves its fuel gauge, so it arrives on the connection that already exists.
  Attaching and removing are read from the strap's own events rather than polled, so the row appears
  and disappears with the pack instead of lagging a minute behind, and a strap that reports no pack
  clears the row rather than leaving the last reading in place. The firmware comes from the strap's
  banked console, which is handed over during a sync — so it fills in after the next sync following an
  attach, not the instant you slide the pack on.
- **The strap's hardware revision is read and stored**, which is what lets a WHOOP MG be told from a
  WHOOP 5.0. A strap whose revision has not been read stays honestly unlabelled — the combined
  "WHOOP 5.0 / MG" — because the two split only on the board, and guessing which one you own would
  decide whether an electrode-only feature is offered.
- **A weekly Sleep Performance card**, beside the weekly Hours vs Needed and Restorative Sleep that
  were already there. Seven slots, a value above every bar, and a night without data keeps its place
  on the axis while drawing nothing.
- **Health Connect, outbound:** skin temperature (absolute reading plus your deviation from your own
  norm) and VO2 max, alongside the heart rate, HRV, resting HR, respiratory rate, sleep and workouts
  already written.
- **Health Connect, inbound:** weight and height now update your profile, so BMI, BMR and the VO2 max
  estimate stop working from a figure typed in months ago. Blood pressure from a paired cuff,
  hydration and nutrition arrive on their own. Period start dates feed the cycle classifier, which
  had the parameter but never a source.
- **Raw frame capture works on WHOOP 4.0**, not only 5.0/MG, and its switch is no longer hidden from a
  4.0 owner.
- **Time in the heart-rate zones, per day.** Read from the strap's own samples rather than a logged
  session, so a day you never started a workout on still shows where your heart rate actually sat. A
  day with no reading shows nothing rather than a zero.

## Changed

- **Dates follow the language the app is actually in.** Twenty-three places formatted a month or a
  weekday with a fixed English locale, so a Spanish build still said "Mon, 11 Aug" on the home screen.
  They read the app's language now — and where your phone is set to a language NOOP does not have, the
  app resolves to English for its dates too rather than mixing an English screen with a foreign date.
- **Sleep reads as a night rather than a table.** The stages are laid across the night against your
  heart rate, so you can see where the deep ran and where you woke, and the stage percentages add to
  a hundred.
- **The Health section that holds the multi-day readings is called Body signals.** It had been headed
  "Skin temperature" since it started there, and now holds the illness heads-up, cycle awareness, the
  body clock and Rhythm Age — not one of which is a skin temperature.
- **The Debug screen is one switch and two exports.** "Enable debug" replaces the separate
  debug-logging and raw-capture toggles, which were always one decision, and its description states
  what the capture writes and what it costs: about 25 MB of decoded records for a night of wear, about
  75 MB for a full day, up to about 280 MB held before the oldest is dropped. The six actions become
  *Full debug export* (the strap log, every capture file and the last 14 days of decoded samples as a
  CSV, in one zip, shown to you in full before it is shared) and *Strap debug export* (the log alone).
  The three bug-report buttons are gone, along with the pre-filled issue form they opened on another
  project's tracker: sharing a bundle is how you report a bug.

## Under the hood

Gradle 9.6.1, AGP 9.3.1, Kotlin 2.3.10, compileSdk 36, Room 2.8.4, Health Connect 1.1.0.

**Every per-second field the strap sends is now kept.** The storage funnel decoded thirteen channels
and discarded whatever it had no column for, and the strap erases its own backlog the moment an
offload is acknowledged, so those bytes were gone for good. They now land in the database: two
further thermal registers beside the skin one, the strap's own record counter, the optical front-end
telemetry including the flag marking a second whose beat detection the strap could not trust, and four
channels whose meaning is not established, stored under names that claim nothing. Nothing reads them
yet — the point is that a question asked later can be answered from history instead of needing another
capture.

**The database moves to v102**, in two additive steps from v100. v101 carries the beat-order column
and the per-second channels; v102 adds the strap's hardware revision, a per-window motion feature
vector, the ECG and rhythm tables, and the coach transcript. Both steps are `ADD COLUMN` and
`CREATE TABLE` only, which SQLite treats as metadata, so the upgrade costs the same on a large store
as an empty one — measured at 5 ms on a 19.6-million-row database, with every pre-existing row count
identical and the integrity check clean afterwards.

**The formulas live in one place.** Sleep efficiency was being computed twice with two different
denominators, one in Rust and one in Kotlin; the Kotlin copy is gone. The same pass moved the sleep
driver tiers, the debt severity band, the correlation cut points, the effort scale, the hydration
goal and the vital-sign bands across, so a threshold behind a displayed word has one owner.

## Known limits

- **WHOOP 4.0 support is decoded and exercised on real hardware**, but only lightly: one strap, four
  days.
- **The battery pack's own cell voltage is not reported anywhere**, so it is not shown. The pack sends
  its charge and its identity; a voltage is in none of it.
- **A pack does not engage a strap that is already full**, so an attached pack on a charged strap
  reports nothing at all. That is the hardware, not the app.
- **Resting HR reads about 10 bpm below a reference band**, unchanged this release. New evidence
  suggests the gap may come from the sleep WINDOW rather than the statistic, since a lowest-sustained
  figure moves with the window where a mean does not — but the overlap needed to test it does not
  exist yet, so nothing was changed on a guess.
- **The ECG tables ship empty.** The capture path is not built — the packet layout and the counts-per-
  millivolt scale are both still unread — so nothing writes them yet.
- **Rhythm Age's scale factor is unconfirmed.** Read it as a trend, not a number.
- **The resting heart rate change rests on one wearer.** The median beat the floor by ten bpm on every
  night where a published figure existed to check against, but they are all the same person's nights.
- **Step estimates on a WHOOP 5.0 are uncalibrated.** Calibration needs a reference step count to fit
  against, and nothing on the phone provides one unless Health Connect steps are turned on.
- Historical nights keep the HRV values they were stored with.
